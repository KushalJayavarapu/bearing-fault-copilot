# Curated Bearing Fault Bridge Table

> **Note:** This is a curated mapping developed for the Bearing Fault Copilot (AMD Hackathon ACT III, Track 1). It links CWRU experimental fault locations to industrial failure modes, physical root causes, inspection steps, and corrective maintenance actions documented in official bearing manufacturer guides. It is not an official OEM manual.

| Fault type | Damage mode name used in the guide | Likely causes (from the guide) | Inspection and actions (from the guide) | Safety-critical step? | Source and page |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `inner_race` | Subsurface initiated fatigue / Spalling | Cyclic contact stress, normal end of fatigue life, excessive dynamic preload, inadequate lubricant viscosity. | Inspect raceway for flaking along rolling track; check shaft runout and journal tolerance. Replace bearing if flaking initiated; flush housing and replenish lubricant. | Yes (De-energize & lock out motor prior to shaft dismount) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 12 |
| `inner_race` | Surface initiated fatigue (microspalling / peeling) | Inadequate lubrication film thickness, contamination by solid micro-particles, high surface shear stresses. | Examine for dull/frosted appearance on inner track; inspect grease for particulate contamination. Replace lubricant; upgrade sealing to contact seals or labyrinths; install fine filtration. | No | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 16 |
| `inner_race` | False brinelling / Fretting corrosion | Vibration or micro-oscillations while stationary (transport damage or standby machine vibration), lack of oil film renewal. | Inspect for axial depressions matching ball spacing with reddish iron oxide debris (hematite). Replace bearing; isolate standby unit from foundation vibration; ensure periodic rotation during shutdown. | No | Schaeffler: Rolling Bearing Damage, p. 28 |
| `inner_race` | Electrical erosion (current fluting) | Stray shaft currents from variable frequency drive (VFD) without insulated bearings or shaft grounding ring. | Inspect for washboard/fluting pattern of transverse ridges across inner raceway. Replace bearing with hybrid ceramic or INSOCOAT bearing; install shaft grounding brush; inspect grounding continuity. | Yes (Verify electrical isolation and de-energize drive circuit) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 31 |
| `outer_race` | Subsurface initiated spalling (load zone fatigue) | Heavy radial loading, localized cyclic stress, housing out-of-roundness, stationary ring localized wear. | Inspect outer ring load zone pattern (continuous circumferential vs concentrated arc). If localized to 6 o'clock position under high static load, rotate housing or replace bearing; verify alignment. | Yes (Support rotor/housing weight before loosening mount bolts) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 34 |
| `outer_race` | Abrasive wear / Contamination groove | Ingress of airborne abrasive dust, metallic particles, broken seals, degraded particulate-laden grease. | Check raceway for polished or matt dull wear track with score lines; inspect lubricant color and particle count. Flush bearing housing, replace seals, purge old grease, verify seal lip lubrication. | No | Schaeffler: Rolling Bearing Damage, p. 19 |
| `outer_race` | True brinelling / Static overload indentations | Extreme static shock load, heavy impact during mounting with hammer applied to inner ring transmitted via balls. | Inspect for indentations in ball track corresponding exactly to rolling element pitch; look for raised rim around craters. Replace bearing immediately; never hit outer ring or use brute force during mounting; use hydraulic press or induction heater. | Yes (Ensure load relief and rigging safety before bearing extraction) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 38 |
| `outer_race` | Fretting corrosion in housing seat | Loose housing fit, housing bore deformation, relative micro-motion between outer ring and housing bore. | Look for reddish rust/powder on outer ring OD and housing bore mating surfaces. Check housing bore diameter and circularity; sleeve or replace housing if oversized; apply anti-fretting paste. | No | Schaeffler: Rolling Bearing Damage, p. 32 |
| `ball` | Flaking / Pitting on rolling elements | Material fatigue, overrolling of hard debris indentations, excessive dynamic radial load, starving lubrication. | Visually inspect balls for spalls, craters, and metallic peeling; listen for cyclical high-frequency clicking or rumbling. Replace full bearing assembly (never replace rolling elements individually); flush lubricant reservoir. | Yes (Tag out drive motor and secure pulleys/couplings) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 22 |
| `ball` | Smearing / Skid marks (sliding damage) | Insufficient minimum load causing balls to slip rather than roll when entering loaded zone, rapid acceleration, incorrect grease consistency. | Inspect ball surfaces for roughened streaks and patches with material transfer; check lubricant viscosity at operating temp. Adjust lubricant to higher viscosity or low-friction synthetic grease; ensure minimum operating load is maintained. | No | Schaeffler: Rolling Bearing Damage, p. 24 |
| `ball` | Overheating and discoloration | Severe lubricant starvation, extreme excessive speed, tight internal radial clearance. | Inspect balls for temper discoloration (golden yellow to dark blue/black); inspect cage pockets for melting or thermal seizure. Replace bearing; verify internal clearance class (e.g., C3 for thermal expansion); restore lubricant supply. | Yes (Allow components to cool below 50°C before touching or service) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 44 |
| `ball` | Electrical pitting / Micro-cratering | Passage of electric current across rolling contact points, spark breakdown of lubricant dielectric layer. | Inspect balls for dull cratered finish or tiny circular pits under magnification. Replace bearing; inspect VFD inverter filtering, motor bearing insulation sleeve, and shaft earth bond. | Yes (Verify zero energy state and discharge DC link capacitors) | SKF: Bearing damage and failure analysis, p. 30 |
